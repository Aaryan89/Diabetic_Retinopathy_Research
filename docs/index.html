Ordinal Neural Networks for Diabetic Retinopathy Staging

Enforcing Monotonic Rank Consistency for Safety-Critical Clinical Decision Support

Problem Statement

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) grading is a safety-critical classification task where standard neural network loss functions fail to capture the ordinal severity of the disease. The 5-point International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) scale consists of: Grade 0 (No DR), Grade 1 (Mild NPDR), Grade 2 (Moderate NPDR), Grade 3 (Severe NPDR), and Grade 4 (Proliferative DR).

The Metric Blindness of Softmax:
In a clinical setting, confusing vision-threatening Proliferative DR (Grade 4) with Severe NPDR (Grade 3) is a minor triage error, but confusing it with No DR (Grade 0) is a catastrophic failure that could result in irreversible blindness. However, standard categorical cross-entropy treats all these errors with the exact same numerical penalty.

Our objective is to implement and evaluate distance-aware, rank-consistent ordinal formulations on the APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection dataset (3,662 retinal images) using a lightweight EfficientNet-B0 backbone. We aim to strictly penalize catastrophic multi-grade misclassifications and ensure patient safety.

Trained Models & Methodology

We evaluated five distinct paradigms under strictly controlled, identical conditions on a mobile NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5050 GPU (allocating <540 MiB VRAM). All models share an identical 5.3M-parameter EfficientNet-B0 backbone. To prevent overfitting on low-level representations during specialized ordinal optimization, Variant D (CORN) freezes early convolutional blocks (stages 0–3, features[:4]). In contrast, Variants A, B, C, and E are fine-tuned end-to-end with all backbone layers unfrozen.

Variant A: Nominal Softmax Baseline

Trained using standard cross-entropy with all layers unfrozen. It optimizes independent nominal probabilities without any concept of rank. The loss gradient treats all non-target classes symmetrically.

LCE = − ∑ yk log(p̂k)

Variant B: Consistent Rank Logits (CORAL)

Reformulates the 5-class ordinal problem into 4 binary classification subproblems. However, on highly imbalanced medical data, negative gradients from healthy retinas dominate the shared projection vector, crushing decision intervals for minority classes like Grade 3 Severe NPDR.

Variant C: Continuous Regression

Maps the embedding directly to a continuous scalar prediction trained with Smooth L1 Loss. At test time, the scalar is clipped and rounded. This method strictly penalizes large distance errors quadratically, yielding low multi-grade errors.

Variant D: Conditional Ordinal Regression for Neural Networks (CORN)

Instead of predicting unconditioned probabilities, CORN models the step-by-step conditional probability of exceeding rank k given that rank k−1 was reached. Stages 0–3 of the backbone are frozen to stabilize low-level edge features, and training incorporates effective-number class weighting and a WeightedRandomSampler.

LCORN(x, y) = [ − 1 / min(y + 1, K − 1) ] ∑ BCE( gk(x), 𝕀(y > k) )

We combine this with a Differentiable Soft-QWK Loss to align training directly with our target clinical metric (Quadratic Weighted Kappa):

Total Loss = LCORN + λ · LSoftQWK

Featured Model: Variant E (3-Seed Ensemble + TTA)

Designed for diagnostic telemedicine where overall classification accuracy and multi-seed stability are paramount. Three independent models are trained end-to-end (all layers unfrozen) across random seeds {42, 43, 44} with regularized label smoothing (α = 0.08) and safe-light geometric augmentations. At inference, predictions are averaged across models alongside 4-way flip Test-Time Augmentation (TTA), with post-hoc logit adjustment available for threshold calibration.

Outputs, Results & Comparison

All models were evaluated on a locked holdout test set of 550 images.

Key Findings

  • Featured High-Accuracy Model (Variant E: 3-Seed Ensemble + TTA): Achieved the highest overall accuracy of 83.82% (vs. 83.45% for the best single seed), with QWK of 0.8859 and project-low MAE of 0.2200, reducing stochastic multi-seed variance (single-seed mean 82.73% → 83.82%) with a catastrophic error rate of 4.36%.
  • Minority-Stage Protection (Variant D: CORN): Elevated Severe NPDR (Grade 3) sensitivity to 65.7% (compared to 0.0% in unweighted CORAL and 41.7% in Softmax baseline), achieving the lowest under-triage rate of 15.07%.
  • Distance-Bounded Robustness (Variant C: Continuous Regression): Held catastrophic multi-grade triage errors (|y - ŷ| ≥ 2) to a study-lowest 3.8%.
  • Clinical Triage Efficiency: Variant E (τ = 0.0) yields the lowest over-referral rate (2.52%), preventing clinic overburden, while post-hoc logit adjustment (τ = 1.0) balances under-triage down to 19.18%.

Comparison to Existing Work

Our lightweight EfficientNet-B0 backbone (5.3M parameters) demonstrated strong efficiency compared to prior multi-branch networks. For example, Shakibania et al. [2] (using ~30M parameters across ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B0) reported 36.84% Severe NPDR recall (different dataset and task setting; not a head-to-head comparison). Our Variant D (CORN) attained 65.7% Severe NPDR recall on the APTOS 2019 benchmark with over 80% fewer parameters, while Variant E reached 83.82% overall accuracy.

Conclusions

Our empirical findings establish four core conclusions:

  1. Featured High-Accuracy Ensemble: Variant E: 3-Seed Ensemble + TTA achieved the study's highest classification accuracy (83.82%), lowest MAE (0.2200), and QWK 0.8859, stabilizing stochastic initialization variance across seeds with a low catastrophic error rate of 4.36% and the lowest over-referral rate (2.52%).
  2. Minority-Class Protection: Conditional ordinal regression (CORN) effectively resolved the breakdown of unweighted CORAL on minority classes, elevating Severe NPDR sensitivity to 65.7% and reducing under-triage to 15.07%.
  3. Distance-Aware Error Bounding: Continuous Regression (Variant C) tightly bounded multi-grade errors, achieving the study's lowest catastrophic error rate of 3.8%.
  4. Hardware & Deployment Efficiency: By prioritizing loss formulation and multi-seed stability over parameter bloat, high clinical utility is achievable on a standard 5.3M-parameter backbone on consumer laptop hardware under 1 GB VRAM.

Areas to Improve / Future Scope

Building upon these findings, future research will explore the following directions:

  • Multimodal Vision-Language Explanations: Integrate our conditional ordinal backbone with lightweight vision-language models (e.g., BiomedCLIP) to generate clinician-facing natural language rationales alongside calibrated stage predictions.
  • Multi-Center Clinical Validation: Evaluate cross-cohort generalization across international datasets (Messidor-2, DDR, EyePACS) to test resilience against diverse demographic populations and varied camera hardware.
  • Edge Deployment on Handheld Fundus Cameras: Quantize the EfficientNet-B0 CORN model to INT8 precision using TensorRT for embedded execution directly on portable, battery-powered fundus cameras for rural screening camps.
